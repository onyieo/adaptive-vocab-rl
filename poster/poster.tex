\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Motivation}
    A conversational language tutor decides at every turn:
    \emph{which vocabulary should the response target?} The turn presents
    a mix of unseen words, words being acquired, and words due for review.
    The choice trades off:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Long-term retention} — words must return before they decay
      \item \textbf{Curriculum pacing} — too many new words overwhelms the learner
      \item \textbf{Conversational naturalness} — turns must sound like
            dialogue, not a flashcard drill
    \end{itemize}
    Real products ship hand-coded spaced-repetition rules. A principled
    RL approach can discover scheduling strategies no fixed heuristic
    matches.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Related Work}
    \textbf{Spaced repetition (FSRS, SM-2)} \cite{fsrs} schedules
    isolated flashcards — no mechanism to embed review in dialogue, no
    joint reasoning about introducing new words.

    \textbf{RLHF-style methods} \cite{rlhf} treat the LLM as the policy and
    cannot reason about long-horizon retention.

    \textbf{Educational RL} \cite{rafferty} works on isolated problems,
    never on item selection mediated through a generative LLM.

    \textbf{Our framing}: a \emph{hierarchical control} architecture
    where RL operates over latent linguistic constraints and a frozen LLM
    realizes them as natural conversation.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Approach}
    \begin{center}

    \begin{itemize}

  \begin{alertblock}{Key Finding: Reward Design $\gg$ Algorithm Choice}
    Same algorithms, two rewards, \textbf{6$\times$ retention gain} across
    every RL method.

    \heading{v3 reward}
    \vspace{-0.3cm}
    $$r_t = \underbrace{\Delta R_{\text{total}}/N}_{\text{coverage}}
            - \underbrace{\alpha \cdot \max(0, n_{\text{new}} {-} 1)^2}_{\text{naturalness penalty}}
            + \underbrace{\lambda \cdot \tfrac{\#\{R{>}0.5\}}{N}}_{\text{retention bonus}}$$
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.95\colwidth]{reward_design_impact.png}
    \end{figure}
  \end{alertblock}

  \begin{block}{Final Retention (500 words, 20 seeds, 95\% CI)}
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.98\colwidth]{headline_retention_20seeds.png}
    \end{figure}
    Every RL method beats every heuristic by \textbf{5--30$\times$} on
    post-gap retention. Online RL (PPO) exceeds offline RL by
    $\sim$1.8$\times$ — the cost of the deployment-realistic offline

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Discussion}
    \heading{Takeaways}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Reward design \emph{dominates} algorithm choice for this domain
      \item Coverage metrics mislead: Random scored 81 acquired, only 18 retained
      \item Hierarchical RL/LLM control is viable: structured directives
            are tractable as RL actions, LLM handles naturalness
    \end{itemize}
    \heading{Limitations}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Simulator dynamics hand-tuned; no human pilot
      \item Coarse action space (21 directives)
      \item Wide offline-RL CIs indicate seed sensitivity
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Future Work (before final report)}

      \item Top-$k$ per-word action space
      \item Extended OOD robustness across simulator parameterizations
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}
