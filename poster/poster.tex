% COLUMN 1 — Motivation (Q1+Q2), Prior Work (Q3), Approach (Q4)

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Motivation}
    A conversational language tutor decides at every turn:
    \emph{which vocabulary should the response target?} The turn presents
    a mix of unseen words, words being acquired, and words due for review.
    The choice trades off:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Long-term retention} — words must return before they decay
      \item \textbf{Curriculum pacing} — too many new words overwhelms the learner
      \item \textbf{Conversational naturalness} — turns must sound like
            dialogue, not a flashcard drill
    \end{itemize}
    Existing products rely on hand-coded spaced-repetition rules. A
    principled reinforcement-learning approach has the potential to
    discover scheduling strategies that fixed heuristics cannot express.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Related Work}
    \textbf{Spaced repetition (FSRS, SM-2)} \cite{fsrs} schedules
    isolated flashcards — no mechanism to embed review in dialogue, no
    joint reasoning about introducing new words.

    \textbf{RLHF-style methods} \cite{rlhf} treat the LLM as the policy and
    cannot reason about long-horizon retention.

    \textbf{Educational RL} \cite{rafferty} works on isolated problems,
    never on item selection mediated through a generative LLM.

    \textbf{Our framing}: a \emph{hierarchical control} architecture
    where RL operates over latent linguistic constraints and a frozen LLM
    realizes them as natural conversation.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Approach}
    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\colwidth]{architecture.png}
    \end{figure}
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{State} $s$: per-word stability, retrievability,
            difficulty, usage; session position. 500 JLPT N5--N3 words.
      \item \textbf{Action} $a$: $(n_{\text{new}}, n_{\text{act}}, n_{\text{rev}})$
            summing to 5 words per turn (21 discrete directives).
      \item \textbf{Reward}: combines coverage growth, a naturalness
            penalty on multi-new-word turns, and a session-end retention
            bonus (formula in Column 2).
      \item \textbf{Methods compared}: IQL \cite{iql}, CQL \cite{cql}
            (offline, \emph{deployable}); PPO \cite{ppo} (online, reference
            upper bound); heuristic baselines: Random, FSRS, FSRS+New,
            RuleBased, SmartFSRS.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Experimental Setup}
    \textbf{Simulator}: knowledge-tracing student with FSRS forgetting
    dynamics over 500 JLPT N5/N4/N3 Japanese words; per-word stability
    grows on successful recall, decays exponentially between exposures.

    \textbf{Episodes}: 10 sessions $\times$ 20 turns $\times$ 5 words/turn,
    with a one-day gap between sessions. Per-turn naturalness scored by
    a frozen LLM judge (OpenAI gpt-4o-mini) on generated dialogue.

    \textbf{Offline RL data}: rebalanced buffer mixing four behavior
    policies plus 100 PPO-distilled trajectories. Reward signal scaled by
    $100\times$ to escape AWR weight collapse at large state dimension.

    \textbf{Evaluation}: post-gap retention ($R{>}0.5$ after a 7-day gap
    following the final session) measured across 20 eval seeds for
    heuristics + PPO; 5 training seeds for offline RL. All comparisons
    use the same env, same reward, same eval protocol.
  \end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{alertblock}{Reward Design}
    The reward combines three terms that jointly target retention and
    naturalness without requiring an LLM in the training loop:
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    $$r_t = \underbrace{\Delta R_{\text{total}}/N}_{\text{coverage growth}}
            - \underbrace{\alpha \cdot \max(0, n_{\text{new}} {-} 1)^2}_{\text{naturalness penalty}}
            + \underbrace{\lambda \cdot \tfrac{\#\{R{>}0.5\}}{N}}_{\text{retention bonus}}$$
    \vspace{-0.2cm}
    The naturalness penalty discourages introducing several new words at
    once (calibrated from LLM-judge ratings). The retention bonus at each
    session boundary rewards words above a recall threshold, providing the
    long-horizon signal that coverage alone misses.
  \end{alertblock}

  \begin{block}{Discussion}
    \textbf{Takeaways:} reward design dominates algorithm choice for this
    domain; coverage metrics are misleading (Random scored 81 acquired but
    only 18 retained after 7 days); hierarchical RL+LLM control is a
    viable architecture for tutor-style applications.

    \textbf{Limitations:} simulator dynamics are hand-tuned without a
    human pilot; the action space is coarse (21 directives); offline RL
    confidence intervals remain wide enough to motivate further multi-seed
    work.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Future Work}
    Top-$k$ per-word action space, behavior-cloning-of-PPO ablation, and
    extended out-of-distribution robustness across simulator
    parameterizations.
  \end{block}
